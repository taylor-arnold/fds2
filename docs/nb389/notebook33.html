cities fresh and build points but leave them in EPSG:4326. In the second
block, run the same join and print the four most isolated by `distance`. Does
the top of the list still agree with question 10?

```{python}
it_deg = (
    pl.read_csv(ub + "data/it_cities.csv")
    .geo.points_from_xy("lon", "lat")

```

```{python}
(
    it_deg
    .geo.sjoin_nearest(it_deg, exclusive=True, distance_col="distance")
    .sort(c.distance, descending=True)
    .select(c.city_name_left, c.city_name_right, c.distance.round(4).alias("deg"))
    .head(4)
)
```

**Answer**: The ranking changes at the top. In degrees, Imperia and Cuneo come
out as the most isolated pair, ahead of Olbia and Nuoro, the clear leader in
kilometers. The join ran without error and returned distances, but in
degrees, which are not a fixed length on the ground. At these latitudes a
kilometer east-west spans more degrees than a kilometer north-south, so an
east-west gap like Imperia to Cuneo comes out inflated next to the mostly
north-south Sardinian pairs, and the order shuffles. The projected answer from question
10 is the correct one. This is the same trap as computing area in unprojected
degrees in notebook32, showing up here as a reordered ranking.
